Frequently asked questions

Who must register with the ODPC in Kenya?

Any entity that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data (a controller), or processes data on behalf of one (a processor), must register — subject to limited exemptions based on employee numbers and turnover. Some categories, including political canvassing, direct marketing, betting, health, financial and telecoms entities, must register regardless of size.

What is the ODPC registration fee?

The statutory fee, paid directly to the ODPC, is KES 4,000 for micro and small entities, KES 16,000 for medium entities, and KES 40,000 for large entities, renewable every two years. This is separate from our fixed professional fee shown above.

What happens if my business doesn't register?

Operating as an unregistered controller or processor where registration is mandatory is an offence under the Data Protection Act, 2019, and exposes you to ODPC enforcement action and difficulty proving compliance to partners, funders or regulators.

How long does registration take?

We typically file within 3–5 working days of receiving a complete file from you. ODPC review and certificate issuance generally takes around 14 days from a complete application, though this can vary with volume at the Commissioner's office.

When should I renew my ODPC certificate?

The ODPC states that a certificate is valid for 24 months and that a renewal application should be made at least 30 days before expiry. We can review the existing registration details and prepare the renewal application with you.

Is this really a fixed fee?

Yes. You're quoted one amount up front for your tier, agreed before we start. No hourly billing, no surprise invoices. The only variable is the statutory ODPC fee, which is set by the Commissioner, not by us.

Not sure if you need to register at all?

Run through our free ODPC Compliance Checklist first — it takes two minutes and tells you where you likely stand before you commit to anything.

Do you have guidance specific to health service providers?

Yes. Hospitals, clinics, laboratories and other health providers face sector-specific registration issues — patient data inventories, data localisation for offshore-hosted records, and recent ODPC enforcement activity against Kenyan hospitals. See our free ODPC Registration Guide for Health Service Providers.

We publish a free, sector-specific Registration Guide for each: technology businesses, schools and education providers, hotels and hospitality businesses, property and real estate, private security firms, and religious institutions. Browse the full set in our Registration Guides library.
